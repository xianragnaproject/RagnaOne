# TikTok Growth Playbook

Operating manual for a new account. Principles first, then the first 30 days, then the weekly loop. Use the RagnaOne app to run the loop; use this file when you need the why.

These notes reflect how creators and analysts described TikTok in 2026: follower-first testing, a higher completion bar, search-like categorization, and a real originality signal. Treat specific percentages as working targets, not official TikTok law. Your Analytics tab always beats a blog.

## 1. How distribution actually works

A new video is not thrown at the entire For You page. It is tested.

1. **Follower test.** Early impressions go to people who already follow you. If they skip, the video usually dies there.
2. **Interest test.** If completion, rewatches, saves, and shares look strong, TikTok finds non-followers with the same interests.
3. **Expansion.** Only then do you get broad For You traffic.

That is why cheap follow-for-follow audiences are poison. They fail the first gate on purpose.

TikTok also reads **spoken words, on-screen text, and the caption** to decide what the video is about. If the first two seconds do not name a topic, the system cannot find the right people.

Working targets for a video that deserves more distribution:

| Signal | Aim | If you miss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completion rate | 70%+ | Shorten, or fix the hook |
| First 3 seconds | Almost no drop | Rewrite the open |
| Rewatches | Any voluntary replay | Add a callback or missed detail |
| Saves / shares | Non-zero, ideally both | Make it useful or sendable |
| Originality | Shot, script, or POV you own | Stop watermarked crossposts |

Likes are a weak signal. A quiet video that gets saved and rewatched can outrun a video with a lot of empty hearts.

## 2. New-account rules

**Niche harder than feels comfortable.** “Lifestyle” is not a niche. “Weeknight dinners for people who hate meal prep” is. The algorithm needs a category. So do your first 200 followers.

**One promise in the bio.** Name the viewer and the outcome. Put the keyword in the bio, not a row of flags.

**Original videos only.** Watermarked Instagram or YouTube reposts get downranked. If you use AI footage or voice, label it in the composer.

**Quality over spray.** 3–5 real videos a week beat 14 forgettable clips. Filler trains followers to skip you, and skippers kill the follower test.

**Expect some deaths.** Under 1K followers, a chunk of videos will stall around a few hundred views even when the work is fine. That is cold start. Do not rebuild the whole strategy after one dud. Rebuild after a week of duds with the same hook.

**Video still beats carousels** for reach. If you use photos, treat them as extras, not the growth engine.

## 3. The three pillars

Pick three recurring lanes and rotate them. Example for a cooking account:

1. 15-minute dinners (utility)
2. Grocery-order mistakes (story / proof)
3. “Save this sauce” (search / series)

Every video should be obviously one pillar in the first second. Mixed-identity accounts take longer to categorize and longer to grow.

A useful split once you are posting weekly:

- **70%** pillar videos you could post again in a month
- **30%** reactive, comments, stitches, or community

Do not chase a sound unless it fits a pillar. Trends are costumes. Pillars are the business.

## 4. Hooks that categorize

You have about two seconds. The hook has two jobs: stop the thumb, and tell TikTok who this is for.

**Use these more**

- **Proof drop.** Start on the result. Screenshot, plated food, before/after, a number. Then explain.
- **Investigator.** A specific mystery. “Why did this $4 pan outcook my $80 one?”
- **Problem → solution.** Name the pain, show the fix in the same breath.

**Use these less on a new account**

- “Hey guys, so today I wanted to talk about…”
- Soft teacher opens (“Let me show you how to…”) with no proof
- Contrarian rage with no payload
- A face talking for three seconds before the topic appears

Put the **keyword on screen in frame one**, say it in the first five seconds, and repeat it in the caption. Three layers. That is search.

## 5. Length, structure, rewatches

Shorter is not safer. A 12-second clip that people finish still only delivered 12 seconds of watch time. Videos in the **35–90 second** range (and longer when density holds) tend to escape small-view jail more often, because they give the system more watch time to work with.

Structure that holds completion:

1. **0–2s.** Topic + proof or question. On-screen keyword.
2. **2–10s.** Why this matters to the viewer. No autobiography.
3. **Middle.** One idea, cut every 2–4 seconds. Pattern interrupt if energy dips.
4. **End.** A save/share reason, then a **rewatch trigger**: a detail they will miss, a loop, or “watch it again for X.”

If Analytics shows a cliff at 3 seconds, the hook failed. If the line slopes down the whole video, the video is too long or too thin. Cut it to the cliff and ship that.

## 6. Captions, text, hashtags

- Caption: one sentence with the keyword, then a soft CTA (“Save this for Sunday.”)
- On-screen text: large, high contrast, first frame
- Hashtags: 3–5. Two or three niche tags, one or two broader. Not 20, not only `#fyp`
- Spoken keyword must match the on-screen word. Do not say “air fryer” and title it “easy dinner”

## 7. Posting and the first hour

Post when **your** followers are online, from Analytics. Generic “best times” are a fallback only.

After you post:

1. Watch it yourself once (rewatch signal, and you will catch errors).
2. Reply to every comment for the first hour. Replies can become new videos.
3. Do not delete and repost the same file. That looks like a repost.

Cadence: **3–5 videos per week**, same-ish days if you can. Going silent for a week resets momentum. Flooding the follow feed with junk resets trust.

## 8. Daily engagement (30 minutes)

The follower test only works if you have the right followers. You earn them by being present in the niche.

- 15 minutes: thoughtful comments on 8–12 videos from accounts your viewer already watches. Add information, not “this is so true.”
- 15 minutes: reply to your own comments, stitch or duet one if it is actually useful.

Do not mass-follow, follow-unfollow, or use comment pods. That is how you collect the wrong audience and a restricted account.

## 9. Profile that converts a follow

- Handle: readable, searchable, close to the niche
- Photo: face or a dead-simple mark. Tiny logos disappear.
- Bio: `I help [viewer] [outcome]. New [pillar] videos [cadence].`
- Link: one place. Newsletter, shop, or a single Linktree-style page.
- Pin: your clearest proof video once you have one. Until then, pin the video that best explains the account.

A follow is a bet. The profile has to make the next 20 videos feel predictable.

## 10. The first 30 days

Do not optimize the account for 30 days. Operate it.

**Days 1–3 — Setup**

- Lock niche, viewer, promise, three pillars
- Film in the same visual world (light, crop, text style)
- Write 12 hook lines before you shoot anything
- Post video 1: strongest proof-drop you can make

**Days 4–10 — Three signals**

- One video per pillar
- Log completion, saves, shares, and where people drop
- 30 minutes of in-niche comments every day
- Do not change the niche because one video flopped

**Days 11–20 — Repeat the winner**

- Take the best pillar + hook pair and make four variations
- Start a series if saves are high (“Part 2” is a gift to the algorithm)
- Pin the clearest video
- Turn top comments into videos

**Days 21–30 — Edit the system**

- Sort all videos by completion, then by saves/shares
- Kill the bottom pillar or the bottom hook archetype
- Write the next 12 titles from the winning pair
- Only now adjust posting times to your Analytics

## 11. Weekly review

Every seven days, answer only these:

1. Which pillar got the highest completion?
2. Which hook type got the first-3-second hold?
3. Which length did people finish?
4. What did people save or share?
5. What will I make four more times?

If you cannot answer from numbers, you are not logging. The tracker in the app exists for this.

## 12. Common failure modes

| Symptom | Likely cause | Move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200–400 views, low completion | Hook or first frame | New open, same body |
| 200–400 views, high completion | Cold start or weak SEO | Stronger keyword on frame one; keep posting |
| Views, no follows | Unclear profile / no series | Rewrite bio; pin a proof video |
| Follows, next video dies | Wrong followers | Stop broad tags; comment only in-niche |
| High likes, no saves | Cute, not useful | Add a list, recipe, or checklist |
| Growth then stall | Identity drift | Return to one promise for 10 videos |

## 13. Tools that are enough

- Phone + a cheap light + a clean wall
- CapCut (or any editor you will actually open)
- TikTok Analytics
- RagnaOne for planning and review

You do not need a growth panel, a bot, or a second phone. You need a repeatable video and an honest scorecard.

## 14. If the account is movies

This repo is set up for a **watch-next** movie account, not a clip account.

- Pillars that categorize: watch-next (“if you liked X”), short runtimes, one-location craft, cheap-looking masterpieces.
- Say the movie title on frame one. Search is how movie-tok lives.
- Lists people can save. Arguments in the comments become the next video.
- Do not upload studio scenes under a new voiceover. That is a takedown, not a growth tactic. Original commentary plus titles is the format here.
- Public-domain films are a later extra, not week one. Week one is four lists people can act on tonight.
